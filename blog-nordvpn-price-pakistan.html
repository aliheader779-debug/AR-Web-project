How a VPN Works — Simply

Normally, your internet provider — and anyone on the same public Wi-Fi — can see which websites you visit. A VPN fixes this with one change: it builds an encrypted tunnel between your device and a VPN server, and all your traffic travels inside it.

  1. You connect to a NordVPN server — in Pakistan, the US, the UK, or dozens of other countries.
  2. Your traffic is encrypted with AES-256, the same standard banks and governments use.
  3. Websites see the VPN server, not you — your real location and IP address stay hidden.

The result: safer browsing on untrusted networks, less tracking by your provider, and access to content limited to other regions.

What to Check Before Buying Any VPN

Not all VPNs are equal. Whoever you buy from, check these five things:

  1. Logging policy — a VPN that logs your activity defeats its own purpose. Look for a clear no-logs statement.
  2. Server count and spread — more servers in more countries means better speeds and more location choice.
  3. Device limit — confirm how many of your devices one plan covers.
  4. Kill switch — without one, a dropped connection can briefly expose your real traffic.
  5. Support — when a server is slow or an app misbehaves, you want a human to ask, not a FAQ page.
