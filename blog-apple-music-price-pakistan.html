What Is Apple Music?

Apple Music is Apple's music streaming service, launched in 2015, and it is now one of the largest streaming platforms in the world. The catalog holds more than 100 million songs — from international hits to Pakistani artists, Coke Studio sessions to underground indie — all streaming ad-free. Unlike some competitors, Apple does not run a free ad-supported tier; every subscriber gets the full experience. The service includes curated playlists made by editors, personalised mixes that learn your taste, Apple Music radio stations, and deep integration with the Apple ecosystem: ask Siri to play a song, hand off playback between iPhone and HomePod, or follow along with time-synced lyrics. Crucially for sound quality, Apple Music includes Lossless Audio and Spatial Audio with Dolby Atmos at no extra cost — a genuine upgrade if you listen on decent earphones or speakers.

Getting the Best Sound Quality

Apple Music's lossless and Spatial Audio only shine when your setup allows it. First, check your settings: on iPhone, go to Settings → Music → Audio Quality and turn on Lossless for both Wi-Fi and cellular downloads; on Android, the same options live in the app's settings. Second, your earphones matter — basic bundled earbuds will not reveal what lossless adds, but any decent pair of wired earphones or good wireless buds will. Note that Bluetooth itself compresses audio, so wired listening gives you the truest lossless experience. Third, download your favourite playlists in the highest quality when you have Wi-Fi, so you are not burning mobile data on the road. None of this costs extra — it is all included in the subscription, which is part of what makes Apple Music good value for listeners who care about sound.
